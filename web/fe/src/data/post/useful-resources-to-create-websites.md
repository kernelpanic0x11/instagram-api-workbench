A professional website is a hundred small decisions: which icon set, which font pairing, where the hero photo comes from, how to check contrast, how to make the social preview look right. Each decision has a tool that makes it easy, and finding those tools is half the work. This is the list we keep coming back to.

Everything here is free to use or has a generous free tier, is actively maintained, and can be browsed without creating an account. Where a resource has a license worth knowing about, it is mentioned. The list is grouped by the stage of the work, from the first sketch to the day after launch.

## Inspiration: what good websites look like

Start by looking, not building. Ten minutes on these galleries before opening an editor saves hours later.

- **[Land-book](https://land-book.com/)**: a large, well-tagged gallery of landing pages. Filter by industry, style or color.
- **[Lapa Ninja](https://www.lapa.ninja/)**: landing pages with the full-page screenshot and, often, the hero copy extracted, which is useful for studying headlines.
- **[Landingfolio](https://www.landingfolio.com/)**: landing page examples broken down into sections (heroes, pricing, testimonials), so you can compare one section across many sites.
- **[SaaS Landing Page](https://saaslandingpage.com/)**: as the name says, sorted by section and by industry.
- **[Godly](https://godly.website/)**: the more experimental end: motion, unusual layouts, strong art direction.
- **[Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/)**: real product screens and flows, mobile and web. The free tier is limited but enough to study patterns.
- **[Refero](https://refero.design/)**: real UI from live products, searchable by component ("empty state", "pricing table").
- **[Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/)**: award-winning sites; more craft than conversion, good for visual ambition.
- **[Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/)** and **[Behance](https://www.behance.net/)**: designer portfolios. Remember that many shots are concepts that were never built.

## Design tools

- **[Figma](https://www.figma.com/)**: the standard for interface design and prototyping; the free plan covers a solo project. The [Figma Community](https://www.figma.com/community) has thousands of free kits and templates.
- **[Penpot](https://penpot.app/)**: open-source alternative to Figma, self-hostable, with design tokens built in.
- **[Excalidraw](https://excalidraw.com/)**: hand-drawn-style whiteboard for wireframes and diagrams. Fast, no account needed.
- **[Photopea](https://www.photopea.com/)**: a Photoshop-like editor in the browser that opens PSD, XD and Sketch files.
- **[Canva](https://www.canva.com/)**: quick social images, presentations and simple graphics when a full design tool is overkill.

## UI kits and component libraries

If you build with Tailwind CSS, these give you production-ready sections and components to compose or copy.

- **[shadcn/ui](https://ui.shadcn.com/)**: accessible components you copy into your project and own; the de facto standard for React projects and the token model many templates now follow.
- **[Tailwind Plus](https://tailwindcss.com/plus)**: the official component and template catalog from the Tailwind team. Paid, but the free previews are a design education on their own.
- **[Flowbite](https://flowbite.com/)**: a large open-source component library on Tailwind with interactive elements.
- **[daisyUI](https://daisyui.com/)**: Tailwind plugin that adds semantic class names (`btn`, `card`) and themes.
- **[Preline](https://preline.co/)**: open-source Tailwind components with a broad set of marketing sections.
- **[Headless UI](https://headlessui.com/)** and **[Radix Primitives](https://www.radix-ui.com/)**: unstyled, accessible building blocks (menus, dialogs, tabs) when you want your own look with the accessibility done right.
- **[HeroUI](https://www.heroui.com/)**: a complete styled component library for React and Tailwind.

## Icon sets

Pick one set and stick to it: mixing icon styles is the most common giveaway of an assembled site.

- **[Tabler Icons](https://tabler.io/icons)**: over five thousand consistent line icons, MIT licensed. The set AstroWind uses.
- **[Lucide](https://lucide.dev/)**: clean, consistent, well maintained; the community fork of Feather.
- **[Heroicons](https://heroicons.com/)**: by the Tailwind team, in outline, solid and mini variants.
- **[Phosphor](https://phosphoricons.com/)**: six weights of the same icon, from thin to duotone, for a more expressive look.
- **[Iconify](https://iconify.design/)** and **[Icônes](https://icones.js.org/)**: search across almost every open icon set at once and copy the SVG or the component.
- **[Simple Icons](https://simpleicons.org/)**: brand logos as SVG (GitHub, Stripe, Slack…), for integration grids and footers.
- **[Remix Icon](https://remixicon.com/)**: a large neutral set in line and fill styles.
- **[SVG Repo](https://www.svgrepo.com/)**: half a million SVG icons and vectors with per-item licenses.

## Illustrations

- **[unDraw](https://undraw.co/)**: open-source illustrations that recolor to your brand color in the browser.
- **[Storyset](https://storyset.com/)**: customizable illustrations, some animated, with several visual styles.
- **[Humaaans](https://www.humaaans.com/)**: mix-and-match people illustrations, useful for team and onboarding pages.
- **[Open Doodles](https://www.opendoodles.com/)**: free hand-drawn figures with a sketchy look.
- **[Blush](https://blush.design/)**: illustration collections by different artists that you can customize; free tier for the basics.
- **[ManyPixels](https://www.manypixels.co/gallery)**: a gallery of free illustrations in a handful of consistent styles.

## Free stock photos and video

- **[Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/)**: the largest free photo library; the Unsplash license allows commercial use without attribution. Their image CDN is what many templates, including AstroWind's demo, load from.
- **[Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/)**: photos and a strong [video](https://www.pexels.com/videos/) collection under a similarly permissive license.
- **[Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/)**: photos, vectors, videos and music.
- **[Lummi](https://www.lummi.ai/)**: AI-generated stock imagery, useful when a concept does not exist as a photo.
- **[Reshot](https://www.reshot.com/)**: hand-picked photos and icons without the overused feel of the big libraries.
- **[Coverr](https://coverr.co/)** and **[Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/)**: free background video clips for heroes; keep them short, muted and behind a poster image.

One rule for all of them: check the license of the specific file, credit when asked, and never use a recognizable person's face for a testimonial they did not give.

## Colors, palettes and gradients

- **[Realtime Colors](https://www.realtimecolors.com/)**: try a palette on a realistic page layout, light and dark, before committing.
- **[Coolors](https://coolors.co/)**: fast palette generator with contrast checks and export to CSS.
- **[UI Colors](https://uicolors.app/)**: turn one brand color into a full Tailwind-style scale from 50 to 950.
- **[Color Hunt](https://colorhunt.co/)** and **[Huemint](https://huemint.com/)**: curated and machine-generated palettes for inspiration.
- **[Palette Maker](https://palettemaker.com/)** and **[ColorSpace](https://mycolor.space/)**: palettes from a single color, with previews.
- **[Hypercolor](https://hypercolor.dev/)**: Tailwind gradient classes, ready to copy.
- **[uiGradients](https://uigradients.com/)** and **[WebGradients](https://webgradients.com/)**: classic two-color gradients with CSS output.
- **[CSS Gradient](https://cssgradient.io/)** and **[Gradient Magic](https://www.gradientmagic.com/)**: editors for linear, radial and more complex CSS gradients.
- **[Mesh gradients](https://products.ls.graphics/mesh-gradients/)**: the soft, multi-color backgrounds behind many 2026 heroes, as images.

## Backgrounds, patterns and shapes

- **[Haikei](https://app.haikei.app/)**: SVG waves, blobs, layered shapes and mesh backgrounds, exported as SVG or PNG.
- **[Hero Patterns](https://heropatterns.com/)**: repeatable SVG patterns as CSS, with color and opacity controls.
- **[fffuel](https://fffuel.co/)**: a collection of small generators for gradients, blobs, noise, shapes and dividers.
- **[Get Waves](https://getwaves.io/)**: section dividers as SVG waves.
- **[CSS Pattern](https://css-pattern.com/)** and **[Pattern Monster](https://pattern.monster/)**: pure-CSS and SVG patterns.
- **[BGJar](https://bgjar.com/)**: animated and static SVG backgrounds.
- **[Transparent Textures](https://www.transparenttextures.com/)**: subtle textures to lay over a color.
- **[Glassmorphism CSS](https://css.glass/)**, **[Neumorphism](https://neumorphism.io/)** and **[Smooth Shadows](https://shadows.brumm.af/)**: generators for the effects that are hard to get right by hand.
- **[Clippy](https://bennettfeely.com/clippy/)**: `clip-path` shapes visually.

## Fonts and typography

- **[Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/)**: the largest free library; pair with the filters for variable fonts and language support.
- **[Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/)**: the same open fonts as npm packages, so you can self-host them (Astro's Fonts API uses it as a provider).
- **[Fontshare](https://www.fontshare.com/)**: high-quality free fonts from Indian Type Foundry, a different flavor from Google Fonts.
- **[Fontpair](https://www.fontpair.co/)**: tested pairings of Google Fonts for headings and body.
- **[Typewolf](https://www.typewolf.com/)**: what fonts real sites use, with recommendations and alternatives.
- **[Typescale](https://typescale.com/)**: pick a base size and a ratio, get a consistent type scale as CSS.
- **[Modern Font Stacks](https://modernfontstacks.com/)**: system font stacks that look good on every platform and load instantly.

## Mockups and screenshots

- **[Shots](https://shots.so/)**: put a screenshot in a device frame or on a gradient background, with shadows and rotations.
- **[Screely](https://screely.com/)** and **[Cleanmock](https://cleanmock.com/)**: quick browser-window mockups.
- **[Mockuphone](https://mockuphone.com/)**: phone and tablet frames for app screenshots.
- **[Previewed](https://previewed.app/)** and **[Smartmockups](https://smartmockups.com/)**: 3D device mockups and scenes.
- **[Carbon](https://carbon.now.sh/)** and **[ray.so](https://ray.so/)**: beautiful code screenshots for posts and social cards.

## Image optimization, favicons and social images

- **[Squoosh](https://squoosh.app/)**: compress and convert images in the browser, comparing formats side by side. The tool to learn what WebP and AVIF actually do.
- **[TinyPNG](https://tinypng.com/)**: batch compression of PNG, JPEG and WebP.
- **[SVGOMG](https://jakearchibald.github.io/svgomg/)**: optimize SVG files, with a live preview of what each option removes.
- **[remove.bg](https://www.remove.bg/)**: background removal for product and portrait photos.
- **[RealFaviconGenerator](https://realfavicongenerator.net/)** and **[favicon.io](https://favicon.io/)**: every favicon size and format from one image or a couple of letters.
- **[OpenGraph.xyz](https://www.opengraph.xyz/)** and **[Meta Tags](https://metatags.io/)**: preview how a page will look when shared on social networks and chat apps, and generate the tags.

## Animation

- **[Motion](https://motion.dev/)**: a modern animation library for the web with a small footprint.
- **[LottieFiles](https://lottiefiles.com/)**: vector animations that play at any size; thousands are free.
- **[Rive](https://rive.app/)**: interactive animations that respond to state, with a free editor.
- **[Animate.css](https://animate.style/)** and **[Animista](https://animista.net/)**: ready-made CSS animations to copy.
- **[Lordicon](https://lordicon.com/)**: animated icons, with a free set.

Whatever you use, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, keep hero animations under a second, and never animate the thing the visitor is trying to read.

## Accessibility checks

- **[WebAIM Contrast Checker](https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/)**: the reference tool for the 4.5:1 rule.
- **[Who Can Use](https://www.whocanuse.com/)**: shows a color pair as people with different vision conditions see it.
- **[Color.review](https://color.review/)**: pick colors that pass contrast at each text size, visually.
- **[WAVE](https://wave.webaim.org/)**: page-level accessibility evaluation with the issues marked in place.
- **[Accessibility Checker](https://www.accessibilitychecker.org/)**: an audit against WCAG with plain-language explanations.
- **[WCAG 2.2 quick reference](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/)**: the guidelines themselves, filterable by level.

## Performance and testing

- **[PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/)**: Lighthouse plus real-user Core Web Vitals for any public URL.
- **[Responsively](https://responsively.app/)**: see a page on many screen sizes at once while you develop.
- **[BrowserStack Responsive](https://www.browserstack.com/responsive)**: quick checks on real device sizes.
- **[Can I use](https://caniuse.com/)**: browser support for any CSS or JavaScript feature before you rely on it.

## SEO tools

- **[Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about)**: how Google sees the site: indexing, queries, Core Web Vitals, rich results. Set it up on day one.
- **[Ahrefs Webmaster Tools](https://ahrefs.com/webmaster-tools)**: a free site audit and backlink report for sites you own.
- **[Schema Markup Validator](https://validator.schema.org/)**: check structured data (`Article`, `FAQPage`, `Product`) before you count on a rich result.
- **[Meta Tags](https://metatags.io/)**: title and description lengths, with previews of the search result.

## Privacy-friendly analytics

- **[Plausible](https://plausible.io/)**: simple, cookieless analytics; open source and self-hostable, hosted plans paid.
- **[Umami](https://umami.is/)**: open-source, self-hosted, one small script.
- **[Fathom](https://usefathom.com/)**: privacy-first analytics with a clean dashboard.
- **[PostHog](https://posthog.com/)**: product analytics, session replay and feature flags with a generous free tier when you need more than page views.

A cookieless tool avoids the consent banner in many jurisdictions and keeps the landing page clean.

## Forms, content management and hosting

- **[Formspree](https://formspree.io/)** and **[Tally](https://tally.so/)**: forms without a backend: point the form's `action` at them or embed one.
- **[Decap CMS](https://decapcms.org/)**: open-source, Git-based editing for Markdown content, ideal for a static blog.
- **[Keystatic](https://keystatic.com/)**: a Git-based CMS with a modern editor for Astro and Next.js projects.
- **[Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/)**: a hosted, structured content backend when the site outgrows Markdown files.
- **[Vercel](https://vercel.com/)**, **[Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/)** and **[Cloudflare Pages](https://pages.cloudflare.com/)**: free hosting for static sites with deploy-on-push, previews and a global CDN.

## Guides and learning

- **[Astro documentation](https://docs.astro.build/)** and **[Tailwind CSS documentation](https://tailwindcss.com/docs)**: both are unusually good; read them before searching.
- **[MDN Web Docs](https://developer.mozilla.org/)**: the reference for HTML, CSS and JavaScript.
- **[web.dev Learn](https://web.dev/learn)**: structured courses on CSS, accessibility, performance, forms and images.
- **[Refactoring UI](https://www.refactoringui.com/)**: design for developers, from the creators of Tailwind. The book is paid; the free articles teach the essentials.
- **[Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/)**: the psychology behind interface decisions, one law per page.
- **[Checklist Design](https://www.checklist.design/)**: what a page, a form or an empty state should include, as checklists.
- **[Growth.Design](https://growth.design/)**: case studies of real products as comics; the best introduction to conversion psychology.
- **[Lorem Ipsum](https://loremipsum.io/)** and **[Mockaroo](https://mockaroo.com/)**: placeholder text and realistic fake data while the real content arrives.

## A starter stack for a site in a weekend

If you want one answer per category, this is a combination that works together and costs nothing:

1. **Look** at Land-book and Landingfolio for an hour; save five references.
2. **Sketch** the pages in Excalidraw, one screen per section.
3. **Build** with a template that already has the sections (AstroWind, for Astro and Tailwind), Tabler icons, and Inter or a Fontshare pairing.
4. **Color** with Realtime Colors, then UI Colors for the scale; check every pair in WebAIM.
5. **Illustrate** with unDraw recolored to your primary, photos from Unsplash, the hero screenshot framed in Shots.
6. **Optimize** every image in Squoosh, generate favicons with RealFaviconGenerator, check the social card in OpenGraph.xyz.
7. **Ship** to Vercel, Netlify or Cloudflare Pages; connect the form to Formspree; add Plausible or Umami.
8. **Check** with PageSpeed Insights, WAVE and the Schema validator; add the site to Search Console.

Then read the [landing page guide](/landing) before you write the home page, and the [getting started guide](/get-started-website-with-astro-tailwind-css) if you are building on AstroWind.
